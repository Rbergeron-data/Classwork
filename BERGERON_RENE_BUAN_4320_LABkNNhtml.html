3. By most measures, k=3 is the best model on training data.

K=3 has the highest accuracy at 97.5% correctly predicting whether or not the customer will have a personal loan. This is higher than k=5 at 96.7% and moreso for k=7. This story continues for all the metrics of model effectiveness. If the bank were concerned about not wasting resources to prepare and send a loan offer out, we would need to look specifically at the the positive predictive value. This is a measure of how accurate the model is at predicting the positive class which in our case is “1” meaning that the customer has a personal loan. The k=7 model excels at this metric at 99.5% accuracy at predicting the positve class. This means that only .5% of the positive predictions were false positives. The k=7 model would be the safest model to use if Universal Bank is aiming to decrease wasted resources on loan offers that are rejected. This model however shows a lower accuracy in predicting customers that do not have a personal loan. This is shown by the recall of 60.1%. This means that 39.9% of the customers that would have accepted the offer were missed. What this means for Universal Bank is that they may have very high acceptance rate for personal loans, which means they are running lean operations, but they are missing opportunities for that would have been predicted. For illustration purposes we can compare the classification matrices. for k=3 would have missed 70 loan acceptances while offering 4 loan offers that were rejected. For the same training data, the k=7 model would have missed 123 potential loans while only offering 1 loand that would have ben rejected. Without knowing the costs associated with preparing the loan offers, vs the additional revenue, I cannot say for sure which model the Cost Benefit Analyses (CBA) supports. I assume that the missed potential revenue of 53 loans outweighs the costs of sending out 3 more loan offers that would have been rejected. For the CBA to favor leaner operations, the cost of sending out loan offers would be over 17x as expensive as the revenues obtained from those additional 53 acceptances.
